Should the phone web app look like the iPhone app?

Yes for its shape, because the iPhone design is the best phone structure dsul has: the same tabs, the same capture bar, the same places to find things. No for its chrome: the web keeps dsul's own Look themes and uses web gestures where a browser lacks the native ones.

Round 3 · the middle ground

The iPhone app's chrome around the web's own views

The frame is the iPhone app's: the inline title with the date and layout, the layout capsule and avatar, the capture bar, the floating tab pill with its Search circle, and the braindump sheet. Everything inside the frame is the web app's real phone views, with the same schedule blocks, mono times and rows as today. Lime marks the selected tab and the + so it stays at full strength in both themes.

Round 2 · web dock (superseded by round 3)

Same structure, real web components, and a Compact density

These are the app's real phone views rendered with sample data, so the schedule blocks, mono times, square checkboxes and header card are exactly what ships today. Only the dock is new. The hand-drawn round 1 boards further down show the structure, and this round replaces their look. The new dock keeps the web dock's well, radius-10 keys and key shadows instead of iOS capsules, and holds the capture bar plus Today, Ask, Organize and Search.

Comfortable (1st and 3rd) gives the dock two rows: the capture bar, then labelled tab keys. Compact (2nd and 4th) folds the dock into one row of icon keys with a short Add bar, about 50px back for the day. List rows drop from 40px to 30px, so the same day takes about a third less height. Schedule already fits the hours to the screen, so Compact gives its blocks more room instead.

Buckets in Compact drops the bucket cards for hairline dividers and tighter rows, which is the iOS Compact idea in the web's style. The last two are Compact in Night. Density would be its own setting next to Mode and theme under Look, phone only, so it works in light and dark alike.

Judged on its own as a web app, today's phone shell hides its surfaces behind a "Go to" sheet and a swipe, and has no Organize. The iPhone boards (D to H) solve both. What carries over is their structure: Today · Ask · Organize plus Search, a capture bar that opens the braindump, and a layout capsule beside the avatar. Browser chrome, touch gestures and installing are where the web needs its own answers, and those are below.

Mockups below are drawn in the web app's Paper and Night looks. Nothing is built: the next step is your pick on the four questions.

Four things to decide

A

Move mobile web to the iOS structure?

Braindump stops being a tab and becomes the capture bar plus a sheet. Chat becomes the Ask tab. Organize gets a tab.

Recommend yes.

B

Drop the week strip?

The iPhone app has none: tapping the title opens Go to date, and a Today button shows when you're on another day. Keeping the strip makes it one-tap day hopping, but the two apps then differ.

Recommend drop it, to match.

C

Ask with nothing connected: show setup or hide the tab?

Today the chat tab disappears without a model. Showing a setup screen (Connect a model or Pair OpenClaw) keeps three tabs. It shows only when nothing is connected. If you turned chat off on this device the tab hides, and a failing key gets its repair screen, not this one.

Recommend show setup.

D

In a browser tab, how should the bottom fold?

Two rows plus Safari's bar is a lot. Option 1: two rows at rest that fold to one row as you scroll, which is what the iPhone app does. Option 2: always one row in a browser tab.

Recommend fold on scroll, like the app.

Round 1 · Board 1

One structure, three frames

Installed to the Home Screen: the app's two rows, capture bar over the tab bar. Anytime stays first, as the web does today. Safari tab at rest: the same two rows above Safari's bar. Safari tab scrolled (question D): the tab bar shrinks to the current tab and the capture bar sits inline, which is the iPhone app's own minimized state. Android Chrome, List layout: the address bar is on top, so nothing stacks at the bottom. List gets the iPhone app's filter chips (All, Tasks, Habits, one per project).

Board 2

The capsule and the title

The capsule works as it does on iPhone and on the web today: a tap steps List → Buckets → Schedule, a sideways swipe steps too, and holding it opens the menu (a long-press with the browser's callout turned off, since nothing drags from the capsule). The menu has Layout and Show (Day now, Week once the iPhone app has it). web Display… adds group, sort and filter, which the web has and the iPhone app doesn't. The title is a plain button that opens Go to date. On another day a lime Today button jumps back.

Board 3

Capture, the braindump sheet, and putting a thought on an hour

The capture bar has two targets, as on iPhone. The field opens a capture sheet that stays open after Return; the tab bar hides while the keyboard is up. The count switches Today to Schedule and opens the braindump sheet over it, with peek, half and full heights. Hold a thought and the sheet slides down to a peek so you can drop it on an hour. Tap a thought's ⋯ for today's schedule sheet, with every current action kept. new It gets an "At a time" row, because a touch drag in a browser is the least dependable gesture the app has.

Board 4

Ask, Organize and Search

Ask is today's chat with the Ask board's header (left today, scheduled, needs you). The composer replaces the capture bar, and proposal cards keep their shipped wording (Do all of it, Not now, Something else). The header names the answerer: AI and the model, or OpenClaw. Nothing connected is question C. Organize lists routines, seasons, goals and projects, plus Item types and Trash from the console. Rows open the existing container pages. With Organize switched off in Extensions the tab stays and says so, and Goals appears only when Goals is on. Search is the ⌘K launcher laid out full-screen for the phone, with its four modes as chips; "? Ask" lands in the Ask tab.

Board 5

Installing, so reminders reach the phone

iPhone in Safari: web push only reaches Home Screen apps, so the reminders page says how to add dsul and marks push as needing it. Android Chrome: notifications work in the browser, with Done and Snooze 15m, and Chrome offers an install prompt the page can show as an Install button. Once the Android app ships, that button can point to it instead.

Where mobile web has to differ

AreaiPhone appMobile web
Bottom chromeCapture bar over the tab bar; minimizes on scrollSame, above the browser's own bar in a tab (question D). The tab bar hides while the keyboard is up.
Long-pressContext menus everywhereOn rows a long-press is a drag (250ms), so menus open from the ⋯ button. Callouts and text selection are switched off on the chrome and on rows, but the Ask transcript stays selectable.
SwipesNo swipe between tabsThe sideways tab swipe goes. It fights Safari's back swipe and Android's gesture back. Pull-to-refresh is switched off on the shell.
BackSheets close with a swipe downAndroid back and browser back close the open sheet or menu first, then return to Today, then leave. Every sheet keeps a visible Close for the installed iPhone app, which has no back gesture.
NotificationsPush with actions, Focus as a Live ActivityiPhone: only once installed, and probably without action buttons, so tapping opens the item with Done and Snooze at the top (to check on a device). Android: in the browser, with Done and Snooze 15m on single-item cues. No Live Activity: Focus stays an in-app room.
Sign-inApple, Google, email linkThe same three. In the installed iPhone app an emailed link opens in Safari, which can't finish the app's sign-in, so the app asks for a 6-digit code from the same email.
InstallingApp StoreBoard 5: an Install button on Android, Add to Home Screen steps on iPhone. The web manifest's colors and icons get a pass to match Look themes.
OfflineNative app shellThe service worker only handles push today. An installed app opened offline needs a cached shell, and capture has to queue or say it can't save.
WeekDay only so far (Week is on the boards)Matches: day only, and Week arrives on both together.

What else moves if you say yes

  • Everything the dock carries today gets a new place. Past-due and undo lines go above the capture bar. The catch-up card stays on Today when nothing can answer. The evening review notice and the Display shelf go under the top row.
  • Places that open the Braindump tab today now open the sheet: the "Go to Braindump" command, three onboarding tour steps and the end-to-end test helpers.
  • Item detail follows the iPhone H board and the approved item conversations design when that ships. Until then, tapping an item works as it does now.
  • The end-of-day review cards open from the evening nudge and from Search, as on iPhone. A Comfortable/Compact density setting and the first-run steps can follow the iPhone app later. Neither blocks this.
