# Braindump that sits: brainstorm (2026-10-07)

## What your list shows

Your 15 items look equal on screen but they are very different animals:

- **Quick calls and emails** (5 to 15 min): dentist, MRI, email ICA, check Sammy's insurance, ask mom and the sibs.
- **Errands** (leave the house): smog the Honda, Sammy's license.
- **Chores**: clean Spencer's room, hang up trip map, cancel subscriptions.
- **Projects** (many steps, weeks): plan trip to Europe, plan trip to China, make China trip video.
- **Fuzzy** (no clear first move): budgets?, figure out CPAP machine.

The overwhelm mostly comes from the projects and the fuzzy ones sitting next to the 5-minute calls with the same weight. The quick ones get lost, and the fuzzy ones make the whole list feel heavy.

Items marked ★ are my top picks. "AI" means it needs a connected model; everything else works with "No AI, thanks".

## 1. Make the list less heavy to look at

- ★ **Size chips.** One tap on a row sets quick / errand / big / fuzzy (or S M L ?). The braindump can then group by size, so "quick ones (5)" sits on top as an easy win. AI can pre-guess sizes for you to confirm, but the chips work without it.
- ★ **Quick wins view.** A button like "I have 15 minutes" that shows only the quick items. Starting with something small builds momentum for the rest.
- **Show fewer at once.** Fold the list to the top 5 with "and 10 more", so you see a doable handful instead of a wall.
- **Projects move out.** A "Make this a goal" verb for "plan trip to Europe": it becomes a goal (the containers already exist) with milestones, and leaves the braindump. The braindump then holds only things you can actually do in one sitting.
- **Cluster suggestions.** "5 of these look like trip stuff, put them in a Trips project?" Same for Sammy. Groups read as 4 topics instead of 15 tasks. AI for the suggestion; manual grouping already works.

## 2. Turn fuzzy into a first step

- ★ **First step line.** A small line under the title: "budgets?" gets "list every subscription and bill from last month's statement". "Figure out CPAP machine" gets "call the sleep clinic and ask what's covered". It shows right in the braindump, and ticking it moves you along. AI can draft it (Break it down and Help me start exist already per item), or you type it yourself when a row is marked fuzzy.
- **Waiting on.** "Ask mom and the sibs about NJ trip": once you've asked, mark it "waiting on mom" so it leaves the active pile until a date you pick or until you mark it back.

## 3. Get things moving

- ★ **Weekly sweep.** An opt-in ritual (could live in the end-of-day review once a week, or Sunday): the braindump shows one item at a time, oldest first, with four big buttons: **this week** (pick a day), **someday**, **break it down**, **let it go**. Fifteen items takes about two minutes, and nothing just sits unseen. No AI needed; AI could add a suggestion per card.
- ★ **Daily pick.** Each morning the braindump offers 1 to 3 items ("today's pick") to drag into today, mixing one quick one with one older one. "Not today" sends it back without guilt. Could be a Today checklist card or a nudge in the dock.
- **Batch block.** Select the dentist, MRI and insurance rows and "Do these together": one 30-minute "phone calls" block on the grid. Phone calls and errands are much easier in a batch than one at a time.
- **Start 10 minutes.** A button on a big item that starts a short focus timer and opens the item. "I only have to do 10 minutes" is often what gets a stuck thing going. (A focus timer is already on the web's wish list.)
- **A recipe for it.** With recipes already built: "every Sunday evening, put the oldest braindump item on Monday". Could ship as a starter recipe in Make.

## 4. Make it feel lighter

- **Age, gently.** A faint "3 weeks" on items that have sat a while, only after some time, never red. Gives the sweep something to work from without nagging.
- **Compost.** Items untouched for 60 days quietly move into a "compost" fold you can open any time. Letting things go on purpose is part of keeping a braindump healthy.
- **Cleared count.** "4 cleared this week" in the braindump header. Seeing progress makes the remaining count feel smaller.

## Top picks, in the order I'd build

1. Weekly sweep (biggest behaviour change, no AI).
2. Size chips plus the quick wins view (makes the list instantly less scary).
3. First step line (unsticks the fuzzy ones; AI optional).
4. Daily pick (keeps things moving between sweeps).
